How Harrisburg Homeowners Can Extend Water Heater Life

Many premature tank failures start with maintenance that never happened, and the habits that prevent them are simple. Flush the tank every year to clear sediment, have the anode rod inspected every few years, and operate the relief valve each year so it doesn't seize. Keep the temperature near 120 degrees to reduce scald risk and slow scale buildup. If you have a tankless unit, descale it yearly where the water is hard so the heat exchanger stays clean.

Equipment age plays a big role. With regular service, a tank water heater typically lasts 8 to 12 years, a heat pump water heater 10 to 15, and a tankless unit up to 20. Once a heater passes that range and starts acting up, like rust-colored water, rumbling, or moisture under the tank, it's smart to start planning a replacement. Replacing on your own schedule is cheaper and is far less stressful than scrambling for a new heater with water spreading across the floor.

The sooner you call about a hot water problem, the smaller the repair tends to be. A sediment layer that's just starting to rumble can often be fixed with a flush, yet the same sediment, neglected through a few more years, can create hot spots on the tank floor and shorten the life of the tank, turning a simple visit into a replacement. Save our number in your contacts today so help is one call away the moment something sounds or looks wrong. A quick call saves time, money, and ruined floors.

In Harrisburg, we work on every kind of water heater, and each one has a typical way of failing. Gas tank heaters usually give trouble through pilot assemblies and thermocouples, sooty burners, and draft problems in the flue. Heat pump water heaters need clean air filters and a clear condensate drain, and they struggle in cramped closets.
