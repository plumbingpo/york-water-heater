How Red Lion Homeowners Can Extend Water Heater Life

A little routine care is the difference between a water heater that lasts and one that fails early. Our advice for homeowners is a short list: an annual sediment flush, an anode inspection every few years, a quick relief valve test once a year, a thermostat set near 120 degrees, and annual descaling for tankless units on hard water, so problems are found while they're still inexpensive.

Take a look at the age of your water heater. The serial number on the label usually includes the manufacture date, and its age says a lot about what comes next. Most tank models reach 8 to 12 years, heat pump models 10 to 15, and regularly descaled tankless units about 20. If your heater is past that range and also shows rusty water, rumbling, or moisture at the base, it's a strong candidate for a planned replacement, and that's almost always cheaper and easier on everyone than an emergency replacement after a leak.

The earlier you report a hot water problem, the cheaper it usually is. A sediment layer that's just starting to rumble can often be fixed with a flush, yet that same buildup, ignored through a few more years, can overheat the bottom of the tank and burn out the lower element on an electric model, turning a simple visit into a replacement. Save our number in your contacts now so help is one call away the moment something sounds or looks wrong. Early intervention saves time, money, and ruined floors.

What Goes Wrong With Local Water Heaters

In Red Lion, we work on every kind of water heater, and each one has a typical way of failing. Gas tanks tend to have trouble with thermocouples and flame sensors, dirty burners, and venting that doesn't draft. Electric tanks burn out elements and thermostats, particularly when sediment piles up around the lower element. Tankless heaters throw error codes for scale buildup, ignition failures, and flow sensor problems, and many of those trace back to skipped descaling. Heat pump water heaters depend on a clean filter and a free-flowing condensate line, and they struggle in cramped closets. Our technicians work on all four every week and stock the common parts to finish most repairs in one trip.
