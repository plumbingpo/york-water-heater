Clear Pricing

Clear Pricing Before Any Work Starts

Good water heater service starts with knowing exactly what you're dealing with. York Water Heater offers honest, upfront pricing for every home, handling everything from a burned-out heating element to a full tank replacement. Every visit begins with a thorough assessment of your water heater, including its age, capacity, fuel type, venting, and the condition of the tank and valves, followed by a plain-English recommendation with the price spelled out before any work starts.

The problem with water heater failures is that most of them start inside the tank where you can't see them. Minerals settle into a crust on the tank floor, the anode rod gets eaten away a little at a time, and once it's gone, the tank itself starts to corrode. When hot water comes out rusty, the tank rumbles, or a small drip appears at the base, have it checked before a repair turns into a replacement.

Not every visit is about a breakdown. Many homeowners call us to evaluate an aging heater before it fails, and it's a smart move. With a typical tank lifespan of 8 to 12 years (tankless units often run 20 years with annual descaling), a replacement on your own timeline beats a rushed swap after a leak. We'll give you a straight answer on how much life your unit has left.

Annual Flushing

Water Heater Maintenance That Adds Years

Routine maintenance is the most reliable way to keep hot water flowing all year. Regular service prevents failures before they start, flushes out mineral sediment, and catches small faults while they're still inexpensive to fix. At York Water Heater, every maintenance visit is built around your specific heater, covering a full tank flush, an anode rod inspection, a test of the temperature and pressure relief valve, a burner and flue check on gas units, element and thermostat testing on electric units, and a thermostat setting check.

Many homes do best with one flush a year, and homes with hard water may need two. We adjust the schedule to the age of your heater, how hard your household runs it, and the mineral content of your water, which determines how fast sediment builds up and how long an anode rod lasts.

Think of maintenance as protection for equipment you've already paid for. Each visit keeps the tank clear of sediment, the anode rod doing its job, and the relief valve in working order, which lowers what you pay to heat water while easing the strain on the burner or elements. For most families, maintenance costs far less than replacing the heater ahead of schedule.

Sized for Your Household

The Right Size Water Heater for Your Home

Bigger isn't always better when it comes to water heaters. What matters for a tank is the first-hour rating, how many gallons of hot water it can deliver in one busy hour, while for a tankless unit it's the GPM it can hold at your incoming water temperature. We don't simply copy the size of the old unit. Instead, we look at how many people live in the home, how many bathrooms you have, and big draws like a soaking tub or a high-capacity washer.

Households change over time. A new baby, a teenager, or an in-law suite can overwhelm the tank that used to be plenty. Tankless water heaters heat water on demand and don't run out as long as you stay within the rated flow, while hybrid heat pump tanks use far less electricity than a standard electric tank. If you're torn between tank and tankless, or gas and electric, we'll lay out the real trade-offs in installed price, operating cost, venting and gas line needs, and installation work.

Common Problems

The Water Heater Repairs Local Homes Need Most

A cold tap where hot water should be, a burner that refuses to light, a rumbling, sediment-heavy tank, a weeping relief valve: each of these problems has its own cause and needs a different repair. York Water Heater tracks down the exact fault before swapping any parts, whether it's an electric tank that trips its breaker, a gas heater with a failing thermocouple, a broken dip tube sending cold water straight to the tap, or a tankless unit flashing error codes.

The correct fix depends entirely on the part that failed. On electric tanks, it's usually an element or thermostat. On gas units, it's more often a thermocouple, flame sensor, or gas valve. Pressure problems get solved with an expansion tank plus a properly set pressure-reducing valve, and leaks at the drain valve or fittings get repaired before the damage spreads. We also check the thermostat setting, since water above 120°F can scald young children and older adults in seconds. If the tank itself is leaking, no repair will hold, and we'll say so plainly. Any gas smell means leave the house and call the gas utility right away.
