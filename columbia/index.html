Keeping Your Columbia Water Heater Running Longer

The best way to avoid a surprise leak is routine maintenance. We recommend that homeowners flush the tank once a year (more often with hard water), have the anode rod checked every three to five years, test the relief valve once a year to make sure it isn't stuck, and keep the thermostat around 120 degrees, which reduces the risk of scalding and slows mineral buildup. Tankless owners should plan on descaling every year in hard-water areas.

Age matters too. Most tank water heaters last 8 to 12 years, tankless units often reach 20 with regular descaling, and heat pump models typically last 10 to 15. If your heater is past that window and showing warning signs like rusty hot water, popping and rumbling, moisture around the base, or hot water that runs out sooner than it used to, a planned replacement is nearly always less expensive and much easier on you than an emergency one after the tank lets go.

If warning signs do show up, acting quickly keeps them small. A dripping relief valve might point to excess pressure or thermal expansion with nowhere to go, both inexpensive fixes. The same drip ignored for months can wear out the valve and stress the tank until the tank itself fails, and a small repair becomes a full replacement and a cleanup. It's smart to keep our number saved on your phone so you can reach us at the first sign of trouble. Early intervention saves time, money, and ruined floors.

Hot Water Headaches Columbia Homeowners Face

Most water heater failures in Columbia announce themselves before they happen. Watch for a popping or rumbling sound while the tank heats, brownish hot water, a faint rotten-egg smell, a damp spot under the tank, or hot water that runs out a little sooner each month. Those point to sediment buildup, a spent anode rod, a failing element, or corrosion starting at the bottom seam, and caught early, they're usually an affordable repair or a planned replacement. Ignored, they lead to a failed tank and water on the floor. Pay close attention to a relief valve that drips or discharges: it means the tank is seeing too much pressure or temperature, and it needs a professional look soon. We diagnose the cause, not just the symptom, so the small problem never gets the chance to grow.
